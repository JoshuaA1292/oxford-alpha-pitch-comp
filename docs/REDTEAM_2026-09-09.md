## What broke (and the fix)

1. **"34× the best year this business ever had" was false.** Standalone SanDisk earned $1.56bn operating income in FY2014. The correct multiple on the old framing is ~11–14×. *Fix:* base year corrected; headline reframed around the post-contract margin the price requires (84%), which needs no cherry-picked base.
2. **The headline was not reproducible.** The doc said $17.4bn/34×; the screen printed $19.1bn/37× (62% margin path). *Fix:* `analysis/sndk_contract_dcf.py` is the single source; consensus net income is paid directly; tests check the residual algebra.
3. **The 62% "boom margin" was below consensus.** Consensus FY27/28 net margins are ~64%/67%; FQ1 guide is 83–85% GM. The old fade scenarios started fading in FY27, so the $960 base target quietly denied the boom. *Fix:* FY27–28 at consensus in every scenario and every MC path; FY29–31 at the contract floor.
4. **The contract book was ignored.** 10 NBM agreements, 8 customers, $93.9bn floor revenue, >4-year weighted duration, ~80% GM at floor, $16.5bn guarantees, >50% of FY27 bits and ~2/3 of FY28 bits. This is the market's evidence of permanence and the pitch never mentioned it. *Fix:* it is now the spine of the pitch — see case §3.
5. **Prices moved.** $1,533 → $1,764 (+15%) on MSCI and S&P 100 inclusion flows; post-earnings low was $1,258 (6 Aug). *Fix:* every number refreshed to 9 Sep; flows recognised as the near-term against-us and as part of "why the mispricing exists".
6. **Short-interest figure was wrong.** 8.2% of float was a Yahoo artefact; FINRA 14 Aug settlement is 7.68m shares ≈ 5.3% of float, ~1 day to cover; borrow 0.28% GC. *Fix:* corrected.
7. **B-L density oversold as "market dissent".** It sits within ~5 points of a flat lognormal at 76% IV; P(<$1,000) moved 31% → 14.5% in a week of data. *Fix:* re-positioned as "what the market prices" plus sizing/tail-cost tool, with the lognormal comparison shown; the sell-side-target probability is stated with its horizon mismatch.
8. **Hedge was incoherent.** "Long SMH" at β 1.73 leaves you net long semis, and MU (a large SMH weight) falls in the payoff state. *Fix:* partial beta hedge only, sized and explained; the NAND exposure *is* the bet.
9. **No sizing, no tail cost, no path.** *Fix:* case §6 gives numbers: size off idio vol, quote borrow, cost the call protection honestly, state the expected adverse path.
10. **Catalyst calendar stale.** *Fix:* rebuilt from primary dates (MU 30 Sep; TrendForce 4Q26 release late Sep/early Oct; hyperscaler Q3 late Oct; SNDK FQ1 ~6 Nov; TrendForce 1Q27 late Dec; hyperscaler 2027 capex guides late Jan).

## The twelve hardest questions (with the answers we now have)

See case §8. The three that would have ended the pitch on 2 Sep were: "what do you know about the contracts?", "SanDisk earned $1.56bn in 2014 — which slide is wrong?", and "your script prints a different number from your slide". All three are closed.

## What the bull case gets right (and we concede)

- Demand is real and incremental: enterprise SSD is 48% of NAND bits (from 26%), Nvidia's Vera Rubin puts 16TB of NAND per GPU, HDD is also sold out into 2028.
- No greenfield NAND wafers arrive before 2H28 (Micron Singapore) / FY29 (Kitakami Fab 3). 2027 supply growth is node migration and China, not new fabs.
- The contracts change the *transmission* of a price decline to reported earnings for 2/3 of bits through FY31.
- Every dated primary forecast puts the supply/demand crossover in 2H27. A 6–12 month rollover in *blended* contract pricing is at the aggressive edge of the evidence.

None of these change what the price requires after FY31. That is the trade.
